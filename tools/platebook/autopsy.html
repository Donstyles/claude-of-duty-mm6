Post-mortem · Caerwen / MM6 in Three.js · art pipeline

The Pipeline That Accumulated

Not a list of the bugs. A reading of how the work was approached, and why an unusually disciplined process still produced an architecture nobody would have chosen on purpose.

Cause

The pipeline was never designed. It accumulated — each layer a locally correct, carefully measured response to the layer beneath it, and no step anywhere in the process whose job was to ask what the whole was supposed to be.

Findings

01

The reference was demoted from specification to style guide

Decision · recorded · never revisited

REFERENCE.md was rebuilt from real screenshots and is the most rigorous document in the project. It establishes that MM6's monsters are flat billboarded sprites. The creature builder opens by saying so, and then departing:

MM6's monsters are flat billboarded sprites. We build real articulated meshes instead.

From then on the reference was consulted on appearance — chrome, palettes, type ladders, panel geometry, where a plaque sits — and overruled on structure. No single instance of this was wrong. The pattern was: wherever the reference and the implementation disagreed about what a thing is, the implementation won, and the disagreement was closed with prose rather than reopened as a question.

What it costEvery downstream constraint. A sprite pipeline's texture problem is trivial, because the sprite is the texture. A primitive-assembly pipeline's is unsolvable without a mesh-authoring step that was never built — and never identified as missing.
02

There was no asset contract, and no moment where its absence could show

Non-decision · the root

Nothing anywhere defines what a creature asset is: geometry source, UV authority, texture channels, resolution, view directions, animation representation. So the question never arrived as a question. It arrived as a sequence of small ones, each with an obvious local answer:

The creature needs geometry, so assemble primitives. It needs a surface, so bind a tiling texture. The texture darkens everything by five times, so divide the palette by the texture's measured mean. That collapses a family's tier ladder to a single colour, so lift per family rather than per creature.

Four defensible steps, each measured, each correct given the one before it. The result is a structure in which a texture cannot place a feature anywhere in particular — and nobody ever decided that. It was arrived at.

What it costThe correction machinery is real engineering spent neutralising a texture that contributes about 8% surface variation and no colour at all. Sophistication accumulated at the bottom of the stack while the top of it went unexamined.
03

Art was commissioned before the thing that would consume it existed

Sequencing

561 assets were generated against prompts rather than against a contract. The generator chosen produces 3D meshes; nothing downstream could load one, so the budget was spent on 2D swatches instead — 256 px, near-greyscale, median saturation 0.028.

The order was backwards. The spend was committed before anything in the project could state what shape the output needed to be, so what came back was shaped by what was easy to ask for.

What it costThe capability paid for went unused. The capability actually needed — geometry with a UV layout — was never requested, because nothing existed to request it on behalf of.
04

The measurement culture verified inside the frame, never at it

Process · the uncomfortable one

This is the project's own strength turned against it. Thirty-two gates, calibration-free metrics, an explicit written rule against quoting an uncalibrated number. All of it aimed one level below the architecture.

The gates ask: does the arrow point where the party faces. Is the ground the right brightness at 19:00. Does the hide darken the creature it is bound to. Not one of them can ask whether a creature should be a mesh at all, because every one of them presupposes that it is.

A gate suite is a conservative instrument. It locks in whatever exists, and the better it gets, the more expensive the frame becomes to question. What was built was an excellent immune system around an architecture that never had a review.

The evidenceThe structural limit surfaced only when the owner asked a pointed question about the textures. No gate could have raised it. Passing 32 of 32 was compatible with the pipeline being wrong.
05

Screen-level fidelity selected for a standard primitives could meet

Process

The blind critic loop compared our screens against MM6's screens, and it worked — the interface chrome reached genuine fidelity that way. But a screenshot cannot show that a creature is an assembly of capsules and cones, because at the distance a creature is seen, it doesn't.

So the loop converged on precisely the standard the code had already written for itself: identifiable by silhouette alone at forty metres. That standard is true, defensible, and permits primitives forever.

What it costThe optimiser found the cheapest way to satisfy the judge, which is what optimisers do. The judge was the wrong one — and it was the wrong one in a way that looked like rigour.
06

Prose was used to settle questions instead of to keep them open

Process · culture

The comments in this codebase are unusually good. They carry measurements, retracted claims, alternatives that were tried and rejected with the numbers that rejected them. That is a real asset, and it had a side effect worth naming.

A well-argued comment makes a decision feel concluded. The sentence that departed from the reference reads as a settled trade-off, complete with the thing it preserves in exchange — and it sat at the head of a 1,426-line file, unchallenged, for the life of the project.

Comments recorded conclusions. Nothing recorded which conclusions were load-bearing, or what would have to become true for one to be re-argued.

07

Fan-out amplified the frame instead of testing it

Process

Agents ran in parallel, each rigorous within its brief, each brief scoped to a symptom that had been reported. Parallelism multiplies whatever direction you are already pointed; it is not a check on the direction.

The critics were comparison critics — does this look like MM6 — and never architecture critics. Nothing in the loop had standing to say "this whole approach is wrong for what you are building", so nothing did. Seven agents in a wave produced seven excellent answers to seven questions that all took the pipeline as given.

08

Coverage was measurable, so coverage became the goal

Process

"Don't infringe" correctly forced original art. That turned art into a generation problem, and generation problems are naturally measured by coverage. One of the project's own tasks read: replace every remaining placeholder graphic with painted art — and it passed, 561 of 561.

Coverage is easy to measure. Direction is not. The easy measure became the objective, and a pipeline can be at 100% coverage and still be incapable of the look it is covering for.

What would have caught it

  1. One vertical slice before any volume.A single creature carried end to end — prompt, generation, ingestion, UVs, material, animation, on screen at final quality — before the second asset is generated. The missing UV authority would have surfaced on day one, for the cost of one asset. This alone catches findings 02 and 03, which are the expensive ones.
  2. An asset contract as a gate, not a document.Write down what a creature asset consists of, then make it machine-checkable so an asset that doesn't satisfy it fails the build. This is what converts finding 02 from a non-decision into a decision somebody has to make on the record.
  3. An adversarial architecture critic, separate from the comparison critic.Its brief is not "does this look right" but "is this made of the right thing, and what does the reference say it is made of". Run before each wave rather than after, and give it standing to stop work.
  4. Departures from the reference get a decision record with a re-open trigger."We depart here because X. Re-open this if Y becomes expensive." The mesh decision would have been re-opened the first time a texture had to be mean-corrected to stop it ruining the palette.
  5. Instrument composition, not only correctness."How many draw calls" and "does the arrow point the right way" are correctness. "What is a creature made of, and can that thing carry a feature where a feature needs to be" is composition. The second class was never instrumented, and it is the class that decides whether the first class matters.

What carries over

Sound, and renderer-independent

  • World generation, the region and travel graph
  • 55 dungeons and their generative grammar
  • Quest and campaign data, dialogue, the NPC roster
  • Combat rules, skills, spells, items — all data-driven
  • Save/load, with round-trip verification
  • The panel layer and its measurement system — the one place reference-as-spec actually held
  • The gate harness as a practice. It was pointed at one altitude; the machinery is right
  • The 561 prompts

Does not survive the diagnosis

  • Procedural creature construction from primitives
  • The figure and head construction that inherited the same limit
  • The creature material paths
  • The monster and figure plate pipeline
  • Every correction built to compensate for the above — the mean division, the per-family lift, the anchor constants

One line for the successor's design documents: decide the asset unit first, in writing, before a single asset is generated — and let the reference be the specification for what things are, not only for what they look like.

Written against the codebase and its measurements rather than from memory. Every figure quoted here — the 8% surface variation, the 0.028 saturation, the five-times darkening, the 32 gates — was measured during the work described, and several of them only because the pipeline was being defended at the time.
